WWE Wrestlers Impersonation Scams
  
WWE superstar impersonation scams are an escalating form of online catfishing and financial fraud where con artists pose as professional wrestlers on social media to manipulate vulnerable fans out of tens or even hundreds of thousands of dollars.
High-Profile Cases and Tactics

The Rhea Ripley Scam: Pennsylvania State Police reported that a 64-year-old man lost $42,700 to scammers posing as WWE star Rhea Ripley. Other victims have come forward detailing multi-year manipulations involving fake "fan clubs," romantic overtures, and demands for gift cards or gear money that scaled up to tens of thousands of dollars.

The Alexa Bliss Case: A 79-year-old man was famously swindled out of nearly $1 million in retirement savings and family funds by con artists pretending to be Alexa Bliss, believing he was in a committed romantic relationship with the superstar.

Liv Morgan and Deepfakes: Impersonators targeting Liv Morgan fans have utilized advanced tactics, including personalized AI-generated deepfake videos using victims' names, to trick individuals into paying thousands of dollars for fake private meet-and-greets, travel, or medical emergencies.
Warning Signs and Red Flags

Requests for Funds: Real WWE Superstars will never message fans asking for money, gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency.
• Fake Verification and Handles: Scammers use lookalike handles, unauthorized profile pictures, and newly created accounts rather than verified blue-check official profiles.

 Escalation to Romance or Jobs: Fraudsters often build intense, fabricated emotional attachments, promising exclusive access, management jobs, or personal relationships to lower the victim's guard
.
Wrestlers including Becky Lynch, Liv Morgan, and Alexa Bliss have repeatedly posted public warnings on social media, begging fans to report and block fake accounts and reminding everyone that they would never solicit money from their audience.
